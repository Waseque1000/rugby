## The key idea

The laws and core skills connect the formats, but sevens offers more open space and fewer immediate teammates around the ball. Players often need to recalibrate support distance, defensive spacing, kick decisions, and their expectation of how quickly a turnover becomes a scoring chance.

Rugby sevens rewards players who can connect their decisions to what teammates are doing around them. Use this guide as a starting point, then adapt the examples to the squad, age group, and competition rather than treating one tactic as universal.

## A practical way to apply it

Use short-sided transition games that begin with a breakdown or turnover. Ask players to identify their first role after possession changes, then review whether they opened space or crowded the ball. Keep foundational skills familiar while changing the tactical picture.

## What to review

After a practice or match, review one example that worked and one that did not. Identify what information was available before the action, what the player communicated, and whether the next teammate had a useful option. That turns a general idea into a specific cue the team can test in its next session.
