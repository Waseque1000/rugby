# Front-Row Fitness and the Rise of 6-2-2 Packs

The modern rugby front row is no longer defined only by size and raw strength. It is increasingly defined by mobility, athleticism, and the ability to maintain power while carrying the ball or moving into a support line. That change has reshaped how teams think about the role of props and hookers.

## The modern scrum is athletic

A powerful pack needs players who can bind, drive, and stay balanced when the opposition presses. The best units are not just heavy; they are also efficient. This matters because the quality of a scrum is often measured by how well the pack can control engagement and release rather than simply pushing harder.

## Why the 6-2-2 model matters

In a compact match, teams often look for front-row versatility. A pack with players who can cover multiple roles offers flexibility when the team is managing fatigue or tactical adjustments. It gives coaches more options in field position, substitutions, and emotional control during long matches.

## The hidden skill: mobility

The athletes who thrive in these structures are often the ones who can move quickly out of the scrum and into the next phase. That matters because the game rarely waits for a front-row player to settle. He or she must contribute in attack and defense while still maintaining the technical fundamentals of the set-piece.

Front-row fitness is no longer a support skill; it is central to modern rugby.
