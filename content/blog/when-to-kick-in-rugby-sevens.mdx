## The key idea

A kick is most useful when it has a purpose: contest space, turn a disorganized backfield, or relieve pressure. A kick without a coordinated chase can simply hand the opponent time and possession. Law and competition conditions can affect restart and kicking options.

Rugby sevens rewards players who can connect their decisions to what teammates are doing around them. Use this guide as a starting point, then adapt the examples to the squad, age group, and competition rather than treating one tactic as universal.

## A practical way to apply it

Show players three frozen pictures: a compact backfield, an open corner, and a deep defensive line. Ask them to name the intended outcome, who chases, and what the team does if the kick is caught cleanly.

## What to review

After a practice or match, review one example that worked and one that did not. Identify what information was available before the action, what the player communicated, and whether the next teammate had a useful option. That turns a general idea into a specific cue the team can test in its next session.
