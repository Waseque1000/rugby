# How American Colleges Are Producing Sevens Specialists

The path from college rugby to international sevens is no longer an accident. More programs are teaching athletes how to play with speed, space, and decision-making under pressure. That combination is exactly what sevens requires.

## Learning the technical core

College rugby gives athletes the chance to refine passing, contact technique, and game management. A player who can carry hard in fifteens but also cleanly support in space is far more valuable in sevens. Many collegiate programs now teach players to transition quickly between structured phases and open-game situations.

## The value of multi-position training

A lot of elite sevens players are not locked into one role for life. They are asked to adapt to different channels, first-phase shapes, and defensive structures. That flexibility develops in college play, especially when athletes are encouraged to understand the wider game instead of only their own position.

## Competitive pressure builds readiness

One of the biggest advantages of college rugby is the regularity of high-pressure games. The best athletes learn how to manage fatigue, communicate under strain, and respond when the match plan changes. Those are exactly the habits needed for sevens at the national level.

The pipeline is becoming more visible, and it is helping the United States become more competitive in a sport where adaptability matters as much as raw athleticism.
