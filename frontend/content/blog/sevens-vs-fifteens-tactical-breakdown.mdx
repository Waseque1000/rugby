# Sevens vs. Fifteens: The Science of High-Velocity Rugby

To the casual observer, Rugby Sevens looks like rugby played on fast-forward. But beneath the jaw-dropping line breaks and acrobatic dives lies a game governed by completely different physiological and tactical rules.

## The Field Paradox: Vast Open Space

In traditional Rugby Union (15s), thirty players share a pitch roughly 100 meters long by 70 meters wide. Defenses operate in tight, organized walls with multiple defensive pods and sweepers covering kicks.

In Rugby Sevens:
- Only **14 players total** share that exact same 7,000 square-meter pitch.
- Each player is responsible for defending an astronomical **500 square meters** of open turf.
- A single missed one-on-one tackle almost always results in a conceded 5-point try.

## Physiological Demands: Heart Rates Near Maximum

Sevens matches consist of two intense 7-minute halves with a brief 2-minute halftime interval. GPS tracking data from international squads reveals staggering physiological loads:

- **Sprint Distance**: Players log between 1,200m and 1,800m of sprint and high-speed running per 14-minute match.
- **Heart Rate**: Average heart rates consistently exceed 88% to 94% of maximum heart rate throughout the contest.
- **Metabolic Acidosis**: Lactate levels peak dramatically because there is no prolonged recovery between collisions.

## Key Rule & Tactical Differences

### 1. Three-Player Scrums
Unlike the 8-player scrums of fifteens that involve over 1,600 pounds of combined force, Sevens scrums feature only 3 players per side (two props and a hooker). In Sevens, the scrum is an attacking launchpad rather than a contest of pure attrition.

### 2. Conversions by Drop-Kick
In Sevens, kickers have only 30 seconds to attempt conversion kicks, and they must be taken as **drop kicks** rather than placed on a kicking tee. Madison Hughes and Alev Kelter have mastered this high-pressure skill.

### 3. Starving the Opponent
In fifteens, kicking for territorial field position is standard. In Sevens, kicking away possession without a 90%+ chance of regathering is considered tactical suicide. Possession is life.
